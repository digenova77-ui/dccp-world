## 3. The per-jurisdiction battery

### 3.1 United States — Howey and the 2026 joint guidance

**The rule.** No comprehensive market-structure statute exists — the CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633) failed Senate cloture 49–50 on September 15, 2026; the enforcement patchwork stands. The governing framework is the SEC/CFTC Joint Interpretive Guidance (~March 2026) plus SEC staff FAQs (notably September 25, 2026), under the *Howey* investment-contract test as glossed by the Commission: a crypto asset is part of an investment contract when buyers invest money with expectation of profits from the **essential managerial efforts** of others — "the undeniably significant ones that affect the failure or success of the enterprise."

The joint guidance's five-category taxonomy is load-bearing: digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools, payment stablecoins, and digital securities — **only the last squarely under SEC regulation; the first four presumptively not securities.** Protocol mining, staking, wrapping of non-security assets, and *certain airdrops* do not constitute securities transactions. And: **investment contracts can end** — a token may cease to be a security once promises are fulfilled or control disperses. Once a system is functional, maintenance/securing/improving generally do **not** count as essential managerial efforts (Sept 2026 staff FAQ).

**The challenger's best delisting shot.** Three prongs of ammunition, all genuine:
1. **Work-as-consideration.** The airdrop exclusion explicitly does **not** cover "airdrops where recipients perform services or provide other consideration in exchange for the tokens." Unity is work-gated — recipients perform services (work) for tokens. A challenger argues prong 1 ("investment of money," read broadly to include services/consideration) is satisfied *because* of the work-gating. The "no purchase interface" defense is weaker than it looks: no *cash* changed hands, but consideration did.
2. **Profit expectations, on the record.** David's own words — "predictions market," FOMO-driven, price fluctuates — are a prong-3 exhibit handed to the challenger pre-labeled.
3. **Essential managerial efforts / central party.** C9: David's 100/100 authority over the split and the standing +1 grants are centralization facts. A challenger argues the enterprise's success turns on undeniably significant efforts of a central party, and that the system is not yet "functional" in the decentralized sense the September FAQ assumes.

If a US venue lists Unity and the SEC deems it an unregistered security, the venue faces unregistered-exchange/broker exposure — that is the venue's delisting incentive, and it is real.

**The construction's defense.** (a) No cash sale at primary — prong 1's classic form fails; the consideration argument is genuine but novel as applied to merit-gated emission. (b) The taxonomy gives a real classification target: **digital commodity** — "assets deriving value from system functionality and supply-demand dynamics." Unity's value derives from grid work-output and supply-demand, not from a promoter's profit plan. (c) The functional-network analysis: as the grid decentralizes operation, maintenance efforts stop counting as essential managerial efforts — the design's direction of travel. (d) "Investment contracts can end" — the construction has a path out, not just a defense at the gate.

**Finding: PREDICTED — contestable; highest-risk jurisdiction in the battery.** The defenses are real (no sale; commodity taxonomy; functional-network trajectory) and the exposures are real (work-as-consideration carve-out; profit-expectation language on the record; centralization facts). Neither side is struck.

**Gating items (US):** (i) a classification memo placing Unity in the digital-commodity category with reasoning a challenger can inspect; (ii) profit-language hygiene in all official materials — David's "predictions market" candor is honest but it is also evidence, and the battery marks it as such; (iii) decentralization of essential efforts *constructed and documented*, not asserted — the 100/100 authority and standing +1s must visibly attenuate as the grid matures, or the FAQ's functional-network analysis never attaches.

### 3.2 European Union — MiCA

**The rule.** MiCA in full enforcement since July 1, 2026. For Unity's category — an "other crypto-asset" (Title II; it is neither asset-referenced nor e-money) — the regime is the lightest in the regulation: a **crypto-asset white paper**, notified to the national competent authority at least 20 working days before publication, with issuer liability for its accuracy. No prior authorization. CASPs operating trading platforms **must conduct due diligence before listing and cannot list tokens without a required white paper**; ~324 firms hold MiCA CASP authorizations (ESMA register, Aug 2026). Title VI (market abuse) applies to any crypto-asset admitted to trading on an authorized CASP platform — insider dealing, unlawful disclosure, manipulation — reaching *any person*, including **ledger insiders** (validators, developers, node operators); issuers carry continuous inside-information disclosure duties; CASPs must detect and report suspicious activity. The Travel Rule (Reg. 2023/1113) has applied to CASP transfers since December 2024.

**The challenger's best delisting shot.** Procedural, not substantive: *no white paper, no listing.* An NCA or a competing CASP challenges on the missing document, and the challenge succeeds without reaching any deeper question. Secondarily: if any marketing or material ever characterized Unity's value as pegged or stabilized, an ART-reclassification argument opens — but Unity is not the peg (eFuse is, and eFuse is unlistable), so this shot is thin.

**The construction's defense.** The steel form *is* the MiCA posture: publish complete, standards-shaped artifacts to the commons; the venue's own pipeline ingests. C3 (no anonymous flows — every movement Unity-bound) directly serves CASP listing due diligence. The design's native documentation habit (evidence architecture, provenance labeling) is the white-paper habit. Title VI's insider net is manageable precisely because the construction already binds every movement to an identity — inside-information controls have something to attach to.

**Finding: PREDICTED — pass contingent on one concrete item.** The single gating deliverable in the entire battery that is fully within our control: **publish the MiCA Title II white paper** (notified to an NCA, 20 working days before any admission-to-trading request). PROVEN: the no-anonymity construction (C3) satisfies the CASP due-diligence direction. PREDICTED: Title VI compliance is manageable with inside-information controls, which must be built, not assumed.

**Gating item (EU):** the white paper. It is also the VARA whitepaper (see §3.7) and the commons-publication package — one document, three jurisdictions' gates.

### 3.4 Canada — CSA/CIRO/FINTRAC

**The rule.** Three layers, no single crypto statute: FINTRAC money-services registration (AML) for any virtual-currency dealing; **CSA/CIRO securities perimeter** for crypto asset trading platforms — since August 2024 the interim restricted-dealer path is closed to new entrants, and custodial platforms dealing in cryptoassets treated as securities or derivatives face **full investment-dealer registration plus CIRO membership** (capital, insurance, proficiency); fewer than 15 platforms held approved status as of May 2026. The CSA maintains a public authorized-platforms list; unregistered platforms face off-boarding. No retail leverage/margin on Canadian-regulated platforms.

**The challenger's best delisting shot.** Jurisdictional perimeter: the CSA takes an expansive view of what falls inside the securities perimeter, and a speculative, FOMO-traded token is the profile the CSA scrutinizes. The practical challenge is venue-side: *is the listing venue properly registered?* — because in Canada the venue's registration is the listing's license.

**The construction's defense.** Work-gated primary, no offering, no sale — the classic securities-distribution fact pattern is absent at issuance. But the battery is honest: the CSA's perimeter turns on its own staff views, and secondary trading of a speculative token on a Canadian platform sits inside the cautious zone regardless of how clean the primary was.

**Finding: PREDICTED — venue-dependent.** The token's design helps at primary; the listing decision is the venue's registration to make. UNKNOWN: CSA staff view of work-gated (non-sale) primary distribution — no precedent is cited here because none was found; the battery will not invent one.

**Gating item (Canada):** approach only CSA-authorized platforms (the public list); no venue, no listing — the venue's registration is the gate, not the token's white paper.

### 3.5 Singapore — MAS/Payment Services Act

**The rule.** MAS is the sole regulator; digital payment token (DPT) services require a Standard or Major Payment Institution license under the Payment Services Act (9–24 month timelines; SGD 100k/250k capital; local substance required). The DTSP regime (Financial Services and Markets Act, in force June 30, 2025) captures Singapore-based teams serving only overseas clients — licenses "granted only in extremely limited circumstances." MAS posture is stated plainly: **pro-innovation, anti-speculation** — retail DPT trading faces no leverage, no credit, mandatory risk-awareness assessments; incentives restricted. Travel Rule above SGD 1,500; full KYC/AML under MAS Notice PSN02.

**The challenger's best delisting shot.** Consumer protection: a FOMO-framed, predictions-market token is precisely the "speculation" MAS starves of oxygen for retail. The challenge isn't "delist the token" — it's "restrict the audience," which for a network-effects token is the slower kill.

**The construction's defense.** The construction fits MAS's innovation side cleanly: no sale, work-gated, documented, no anonymous flows (C3 maps onto PSN02's AML expectations). Retail friction is the *venue's* to manage under its license — risk assessments, no leverage — and licensed venues (Coinbase Singapore, OKX, Crypto.com, Gemini, Independent Reserve, DBS Vickers, Coinhako, 30+ others) already operate that machinery.

**Finding: PREDICTED — listable on MAS-licensed venues with retail safeguards.** The anti-speculation posture is a headwind on *framing*, not a bar on *listing* — promotion hygiene again decides how much headwind.

**Gating item (Singapore):** venue must hold MPI/SPI DPT license; retail communications reviewed against MAS's anti-speculation expectations.

Travel Rule in effect (no threshold); tax reform toward a flat 20% for specified cryptoassets.

### 3.7 United Arab Emirates — five regulators, one token

**The rule.** Five concurrent regimes (mid-2026): CMA (federal, onshore — Virtual Assets Framework April 2026), VARA (Dubai ex-DIFC), DFSA (DIFC), FSRA (ADGM), CBUAE (payment tokens). VARA's April 9, 2026 issuance guidance — the world's first dedicated token-issuance guidance — sets three pathways: **Category 1** (fiat-referenced / asset-referenced — full issuance license), **Category 2** (everything else transferable, e.g. ETH-like — no issuer license, but **must be placed/distributed exclusively through a VARA-licensed distributor**, which carries ongoing due-diligence responsibility), and **Exempt** (non-transferable, closed-loop). Every non-exempt issuer publishes a **whitepaper and Risk Disclosure Statement** — ranked material risks, no boilerplate. Federal **privacy-token ban** in effect February 2026 (Monero, Zcash).

**The challenger's best delisting shot.** Distribution licensing: a Category 2 token reaching Dubai investors without a VARA-licensed distributor is non-compliant *at the distribution layer* regardless of the token's design purity. The challenge is aimed at the pipe, not the token.

**The construction's defense.** Unity as a transferable non-referenced token is a clean **Category 2** fit — no issuer license required, licensed-distributor distribution, whitepaper plus risk disclosure. VARA's disclosure-first philosophy is the steel form in regulatory clothing: "clear, accurate, and accessible... not legal boilerplate designed to obscure." PROVEN: Unity is not a privacy token — the federal ban is inapplicable by construction (C3: no anonymous flows).

**Finding: PREDICTED — pass via the Category 2 pathway.** The most mechanically clear pass in the battery: licensed distributor + whitepaper + ranked risk disclosure = the gate, fully specified.

**Gating item (UAE):** engage a VARA-licensed distributor; the whitepaper doubles as the MiCA document (§3.2) — one publication package, two gates.
