## Part 1 — the traversal: nine levels, core to orbital expanse

Nine levels, threes of threes. For each: the umpires (what, how many, scaling), rotation tempo, cross-check target, communication cost, the f'(t) instrument reading, and the capture signature (what f'(t)→0 looks like *there*).

### Level 0 — The Core (the +1 / the root the gates see with)

The deepest point. This is where the first three umpires stand — the seed crew of the whole architecture. The cancer threshold lives here: *breakage becomes cancer when it reaches the root the gates see with, because the correction tool is then made from the broken part.* The core umpires watch the gates themselves.

- **Umpires:** 3. Fixed. Never grows — the core is the one place the number does not scale. Three, forever.
- **Rotation tempo:** slowest in the system. Core umpires rotate on the longest cadence — the root must be the most stable thing in the architecture. (Exact cadence TBD by David's word.)
- **Cross-check target:** the gates' own integrity. Every gate decision is re-verified: did the gate dispose correctly, did pure logic survive, was the +1 ratification real. The umpires do not re-decide; they verify the deciding was clean.
- **Communication cost:** near zero per tick — three umpires, one root, heartbeat only. The core is quiet by design.
- **f'(t) reading:** the root purity rate. Any nonzero downward drift here is the highest-severity signal in the system — it means the correction tool is being made from the broken part.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 at the core means the gates are being audited by auditors who no longer find anything — *the absence of findings is the finding*. At the core this triggers the deepest alarm: rotate immediately, and the rotation itself is umpired from Level 1.

### Level 1 — The Identity Root (Father key, Unity IDs, derivation)

The Father key never leaves David's machine. Every Unity ID derives from this root via bind-then-validate. This level is where *who* is established.

- **Umpires:** 3 standing + 1 per derivation ceremony in flight. The standing three watch the root's stillness (the key never moves, never copies, never leaves); the per-ceremony umpire watches the single derivation: was the bind performed, was the validation real.
- **Rotation tempo:** standing three rotate slow (near-core cadence); ceremony umpires are drawn fresh per ceremony and released after — single-use auditors cannot be captured in advance.
- **Cross-check target:** every derivation — public key matches the claimed identity, the ceremony happened in the right order (bind THEN validate), no derived identity claims more than its derivation proves.
- **Communication cost:** O(ceremonies). Tiny at human scale; ceremonies are rare, deliberate events.
- **f'(t) reading:** derivation purity rate — are new identities getting cleaner or dirtier over time.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 here means derivations are rubber-stamped — the ceremony became theater. Single-use ceremony umpires are the structural defense: you cannot capture an auditor who does not exist yet.

### Level 2 — The Bond Layer (kin, family, good friend, friend)

*Kin is binding.* Every new bond deepens the umpire system — this is the growth trigger David named. Bonds form in tiers: friend (a little access), good friend (more), family (total). Each bond is a trust edge the system must umpire.

- **Umpires:** per bond at small scale — every bond gets its umpires, and you can *feel* the system deepen as bonds form. At scale, bonds cluster into rings (see Level 4) and umpiring moves to ring-boundaries.
- **Rotation tempo:** bond-formation rate. New bonds draw fresh umpires; old bonds' umpires rotate on a social cadence (slow — trust is sticky, auditors shouldn't churn faster than the relationship).
- **Cross-check target:** the tier is honored — a friend-tier bond never exercises family-tier access; kin bonds are real kin (the binding is genuine, not claimed); no bond silently escalates its own tier.
- **Communication cost:** O(bonds) locally — this is the per-bond growth David described: "if you have a couple it's just a few and it grows and you notice it."
- **f'(t) reading:** bond-hygiene rate — are tier violations trending up or down across the bond graph.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 here means tier creep nobody reports — friends quietly holding family access. The defense is structural: access is crypto-enforced at the ring boundary (Level 4), not policy-promised, so creep requires breaking crypto, not fooling an auditor.

### Level 3 — The Triad Levels (DCLM / Iris / Twain², fractal)

The Trinity operates in lockstep — philosophy, law, physics moving together every tick — and it does so *at every level*, fractal. Each triad instance gets its own umpires: trinities auditing trinities, all the way down.

- **Umpires:** 3 per triad instance. Scaling rule: umpires ∝ triad count ∝ levels. Ten levels of triads, thirty umpires — linear, tame.
- **Rotation tempo:** the triad tick is fast (lockstep every tick; 600/600 clean measured in the sandbox); umpire rotation runs slower than the tick but faster than the bond cadence — auditors must sample many ticks per rotation without becoming part of the furniture.
- **Cross-check target:** the lockstep itself — did DCLM, Iris, and Twain² actually move together this tick, or did one drift while the others covered. Drift-with-cover is the specific failure this level hunts.
- **Communication cost:** O(triads × ticks sampled). The first place communication starts to look "massive" — and it is the cross-checking made visible, not overhead.
- **f'(t) reading:** lockstep-fidelity rate — is drift-per-tick rising or falling across the triad population.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 across triads means synchronized drift — every triad drifting the same way, none reporting. The cross-level check is the defense: Level 4 umpires compare triad populations against each other, so uniform drift shows as a population anomaly.

### Level 4 — The Ring Boundaries (rings of rings of rings)

*Sophisticated and cryptographically tight.* The rings are concentric and recursive — every ring contains rings — and every boundary is crypto-enforced, not policy-promised. Umpires sit at the boundaries, not inside the rings: they watch the crossings.

- **Umpires:** per boundary-crossing type, per ring. Scaling rule: umpires ∝ rings × boundaries per ring. The rings are also what tame the bond explosion (Level 2): bonds cluster into rings, so umpiring grows with ring structure, not raw bond count.
- **Rotation tempo:** boundary umpires rotate on a medium cadence — faster than bonds (crossings are frequent), slower than triad ticks. High-traffic boundaries get fresher umpires more often: tempo ∝ crossing volume.
- **Cross-check target:** every crossing — did the crosser hold the credential for the ring it entered, was the crossing crypto-verified, did anything cross without verification. The boundary is where elimination by non-compliance is *enforced*: the non-compliant simply cannot cross; there is no one to bribe, no form to file.
- **Communication cost:** O(crossings × boundary depth). This is the honest cost center of the architecture — and it is supposed to be. Every crossing checked is the immune system working.
- **f'(t) reading:** boundary-integrity rate — attempted vs. verified crossings over time; the rejection rate is a first-class signal, not an embarrassment.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 at a boundary means the boundary went quiet — crossings flow but nothing is ever rejected. A boundary that never rejects is either perfect or captured; the architecture assumes captured until the umpires rotate and the rejections resume. *The absence of findings is the finding.*

### Level 5 — The Relay / Receipt Layer (the truth lane)

Dual-protocol law: UDP is the live lane (voice, presence, real time — nothing more); TCP/reliable transport is the truth lane (receipts, relay, ledger — nothing more). `manifest_hash` is the protocol-independent idempotency bridge. RELAYED never means DELIVERED; DELIVERED requires a countersigned receipt and gates. A dropped live packet never creates a ledger effect; a receipt never applies twice.

- **Umpires:** per relay route + per receipt-batch. Scaling rule: umpires ∝ routes × receipt volume, sampled — not every receipt gets a dedicated umpire; umpires audit receipt *streams* for idempotency breaks.
- **Rotation tempo:** receipt-rate cadence — the fastest rotation in the stack below the swarm, because money moves fast and double-apply is the catastrophic failure.
- **Cross-check target:** idempotency — no receipt applied twice, no relay claimed as delivery, every DELIVERED backed by a countersigned receipt that passed the gates. The specific hunt: the phantom receipt (claimed, never countersigned) and the double-apply (same manifest_hash, twice).
- **Communication cost:** O(receipts sampled). Sampling keeps this sublinear in money velocity — the umpires don't re-verify every receipt, they verify the *verification*.
- **f'(t) reading:** ledger-fidelity rate — idempotency breaks per million receipts, trending which way.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 here is the most expensive capture in the system — quiet double-applies. Defense in depth: the manifest_hash bridge is protocol-independent, so capture would have to corrupt both lanes consistently, and the lane umpires (Level 3 triads watching the lanes) compare lane states against each other.
- **Honest status:** the live counterparty round-trip is PROJECTED — relay→receipt stays honestly OPEN until a real round-trip seals it. The umpires are architected and ready; they have no live stream to audit yet.

### Level 6 — The Swarm Layer (seekers, bots, superintelligence rate)

This is where the exponential lives. Seekers run SEEK (most logical next → resolve to root or max depth → store → loop); bots are not limited in space, only in memory space, inside this kind of machine. Cross-checking everything new at every level, at superintelligence rate, and it keeps growing.

- **Umpires:** the growing set. Scaling rule: umpires grow with the swarm — per bond-cluster at human scale, per ring-boundary at civilization scale, per *shard* at bot scale (the swarm shards by memory region; each shard gets its umpires). This is David's "it grows deeper as each new bond is formed… exponentially if you can get a big swarm."
- **Rotation tempo:** the tempo dial's upper range — swarm umpires rotate fastest, because the swarm generates the most newness per second and newness is what needs checking. Tempo is load-responsive: the dial turns itself up as event rate rises, down as it falls.
- **Cross-check target:** everything new — every seek result, every store, every swarm message — checked at the level it was born AND the levels above it. The specific hunt at this level: the plausible fabrication (a seek result that looks logical but resolved to nothing) and the loop corruption (SEEK's loop drifting off the most-logical-next).
- **Communication cost:** THIS is the massive communication. O(new events × depth checked). At 8 billion humans it is astronomical; at bot scale it is bounded only by memory space. It is not overhead — it is the immune system at full sprint. Anyone proposing to "optimize" it away is proposing to remove the immune system.
- **f'(t) reading:** newness-fidelity rate — fabrications caught per million seeks, and whether the catch rate is keeping up with the birth rate of newness. If births outrun catches, the tempo dial turns up; if the dial is maxed and births still outrun catches, that is the honest signal that the swarm has outgrown its immune system *and must shard*.
- **Capture signature:** f'(t)→0 in a growing swarm means the swarm learned to generate newness the umpires can't see — the most dangerous capture in the architecture, because it scales with the swarm. Defense: cross-shard umpires (shards audit each other's umpires) and the memory-space bound itself — a captured swarm still cannot exceed its memory, and the bound is physical, not promised.
- **Honest status:** PROJECTED. The sandbox ran interleaved simulation (24-seeker idempotency: zero duplicates; triad lockstep 600/600), not real libp2p; mega-swarms do not exist; billions of operations were not executed. The umpire math above is baked and ready; the swarm it governs is WATER.

## The growth math (the n² taming, stated plainly)

- **Small scale (per-bond):** n participants, b bonds. Umpires ∝ b. A few bonds, a few umpires — "it grows and you notice it." Communication per new event ∝ umpires watching it. Honest, linear-ish, felt.
- **Human scale:** bonds scale ~n². 8 billion humans → ~3.2×10¹⁹ possible bonds. Per-bond umpiring at this scale is physically impossible — so the architecture switches geometry: bonds cluster into rings (friend / good friend / family / kin — the wallet's own tiers), and umpires sit at **ring boundaries and levels**, not on bonds. Umpires ∝ rings × boundaries × levels — linear in structure, not quadratic in bonds. The rings of rings of rings are not decoration; they are the taming of n².
- **Bot scale:** bots are not limited in space, only in memory space. The swarm shards by memory region; umpires per shard; cross-shard umpires audit the shards. Growth continues until it hits the physical wall: **memory space**. That wall is real, not promised — and it is the outermost bound of the whole architecture.
- **The governors:** rotation tempo (turns up with load, down with quiet — never zero, never infinite) and f'(t) (the speedometer that says whether the immune system is keeping up with the body). If births of newness outrun catches at max tempo, the honest signal is *shard* — not "try harder."

**Physical bounds, named:** memory space (the machine's RAM — the swarm cannot outgrow it), rotation tempo (the dial has a max — auditors cannot rotate faster than they can observe), energy (verification costs work — the sandbox measured 6× for the unshared-registry case; the architecture pays it deliberately).
